# STATIC EQUIPMENT INSPECTION REPORT: E-302 CRUDE PRE-HEAT EXCHANGER
**Document ID:** DOC-INSP-2026-E302
**Date of Inspection:** 2026-01-20
**Equipment Tag:** E-302 A/B (Crude Distillation Unit Shell-and-Tube Exchanger)
**Service:** Shell side: Atmospheric Residue (Heavy Stream) | Tube side: Desalted Crude Oil
**Inspection Code:** API 510 Pressure Vessel Inspection Code
**Lead Inspector:** P. Anantharaman (API Certified Inspector #510-48291)

---

## 1. PURPOSE & OPERATIONAL BACKGROUND
Exchanger E-302 was scheduled for internal bundle pull and non-destructive examination (NDE) due to persistent thermal efficiency loss and high pressure drop across the shell side. Shell side differential pressure had increased from baseline 0.8 bar to 2.45 bar, restricting crude throughput by approximately 12%.

## 2. INSPECTION FINDINGS
- **Tube Bundle Internal & External Condition:**
  - Shell-side (Atmospheric residue): Heavy carbonaceous asphaltene and coke deposits covering 85% of outer tube surfaces, with average deposit thickness of 3.5 mm in the first 4 baffle compartments.
  - Tube-side (Crude): Light inorganic salt fouling (primarily calcium chloride and clay fines) near inlet channel head.
- **Wall Thickness & Eddy Current Testing (ECT):**
  - Total tubes inspected via Internal Rotary Inspection System (IRIS) and ECT: 1,420 carbon steel tubes (OD 19.05 mm, BWG 14, nominal wall thickness: 2.11 mm).
  - Minimum remaining wall thickness observed: **1.82 mm** (Retirement limit: 1.40 mm; remaining life estimated at 6.8 years at current corrosion rate of 0.042 mm/year).
  - 14 tubes in the top row directly below inlet impingement plate showed localized wall thinning due to erosion-corrosion. These 14 tubes were plugged with tapered mechanical alloy plugs.
- **Shell & Channel Shell Assessment:**
  - Shell shell minimum measured wall thickness: 15.8 mm (Nominal: 16.0 mm, Minimum required: 13.2 mm).
  - Baffle plates exhibited minor edge fretting wear; baffle hole clearances measured 20.1 mm (within allowable TEMA tolerance).

## 3. CLEANING & REPAIR WORK
1. Hydro-jetting: Shell side high-pressure hydro-milling at 1,400 bar (20,000 PSI) using rotating cleaning nozzles; recovered approximately 450 kg of hard coked residue.
2. Chemical circulation: 4-hour hot wash with biodegradable aromatic solvent blend to strip sub-micron organic films.
3. Tube plugging: 14 tubes plugged with 316L SS expandable tapered plugs.
4. Gasket replacement: Installed new spiral wound gaskets (316L SS with flexible graphite filler, ASME B16.20) on floating head and channel covers.

## 4. POST-CLEANING HYDROSTATIC TEST
- Hydrostatic test pressure: **32.5 bar** (Shell side) and **48.0 bar** (Tube side) held for 60 minutes with zero pressure drop and zero leak detection across tube-to-tubesheet seal welds.

## 5. RETURN TO SERVICE RESULTS
- Heat transfer coefficient ($U$-value) restored from 185 W/m²·K back to design **340 W/m²·K** (+83% improvement).
- Shell-side pressure drop reduced from 2.45 bar to **0.78 bar**.
- Overall crude pre-heat train temperature increase at furnace inlet: **+14.5°C**, generating annual fuel gas savings estimated at synthetic benchmark value.
